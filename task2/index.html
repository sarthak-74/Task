01

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a form of cyberattack in which attackers impersonate trusted individuals or organizations to trick people into revealing information or performing unsafe actions.

📧

Email Phishing

Fraudulent emails designed to trick users into clicking malicious links, opening attachments, or revealing sensitive information.

📱

Smishing

Phishing attacks delivered through SMS or messaging applications.

☎️

Vishing

Voice-based phishing where attackers use phone calls to impersonate trusted organizations.

🌐

Website Phishing

Fake websites designed to imitate legitimate services and steal credentials or financial data.

02

How to Spot Phishing

Attackers often use urgency, fear, impersonation, and deception to manipulate their targets.

01

Suspicious Sender

Check the complete email address. Attackers may use addresses that look similar to legitimate ones.

02

Urgent Language

Be suspicious of messages demanding immediate action or threatening account suspension.

03

Unexpected Links

Don't click unexpected links. Verify the destination before opening it.

04

Requests for Secrets

Legitimate organizations generally do not ask for passwords, OTPs, or recovery codes through unexpected messages.

05

Suspicious Attachments

Unexpected attachments may contain malicious content. Verify the sender before opening them.

06

Poor Grammar or Branding

Spelling mistakes, unusual formatting, or inconsistent branding can be warning signs.

04

Social Engineering Techniques

Social engineering attacks manipulate human behavior rather than relying only on technical vulnerabilities.

😨

Fear

Attackers may threaten account closure, financial loss, or security consequences.

⏰

Urgency

Victims are pressured to act quickly without verifying the request.

🎁

Reward

Fake prizes, discounts, refunds, or job offers may be used to attract victims.

👤

Impersonation

Attackers may pretend to be managers, banks, technical support, or other trusted parties.

05

Real-World Case Studies

Understanding real incidents helps demonstrate how phishing can affect individuals and organizations.

CASE STUDY 01

Google and Facebook Business Email Scam

A well-known fraud case involved an attacker impersonating legitimate businesses and using fraudulent invoices and communications to convince employees to transfer money.

Key Lesson: Financial requests should be independently verified, especially when they involve unusual payment instructions.
CASE STUDY 02

Credential Phishing

Attackers frequently create fake authentication pages that imitate commonly used services. Victims who enter credentials may unknowingly provide them to attackers.

Key Lesson: Always verify the website address before entering credentials and use multi-factor authentication.
CASE STUDY 03

Business Email Compromise

Business Email Compromise attacks can involve impersonating executives, suppliers, or financial departments to request sensitive information or payments.

Key Lesson: Verify unusual requests through an independent communication channel.

Stay Safe Online

Follow these practical security habits.

✓

Think before clicking unexpected links.

✓

Verify suspicious requests using another channel.

✓

Use strong, unique passwords.

✓

Enable multi-factor authentication.

✓

Keep your operating system and applications updated.

✓

Never share passwords or authentication codes.

✓

Report suspicious messages to your organization.

✓

When in doubt, stop and verify.
